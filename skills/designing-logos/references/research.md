# Reference research and similarity checks

Read this at the start of P3 (after G3) and again before G6 when the mark will be registered. Reference work gives principles, never shapes.

## Rules

1. Look at existing marks only after the meaning grid is done. People who see examples first copy their features, flaws included (Jansson and Smith 1991); pictures are copied more readily than words (Purcell and Gero 1996). Before G3 the only images the user sees are your own L3 cards.
2. Prefer references from other fields and other periods than the brand's own. They give principles that do not look like the competitors.
3. Store screenshots and links only. Do not download or re-save other people's work, and never put it in a deliverable.
4. A reference earns a place only through a principle card with at least one principle you can apply. No principle, no card.
5. Every direction names the card and principle it uses. The nine-grid cat used four from one card (Nick Zotov's collection): local pixels only, repeated modules, a second meaning in the negative space, solid single color.

Check: `references.md` has three to six cards, each with "can take" and "must not copy", and every direction in `directions.json` lists the principles it used.

## Search terms

Build three to five queries of the form subject x form operation x tradition, each in at least two languages. For a cat mark: `cat logomark negative space`, `猫 ロゴマーク 幾何学`, `家紋 猫`, `Katze Signet`. Add the style recipe's own terms (style-recipes.md) when one was chosen in the brief.

Open a source's front page first and note whether it is readable, needs a login, or blocks you. Skip blocked sources and tell the user which ones.

## Principle cards

One card per reference, in `references.md`:

```
### Card 2: <designer or source, work, year> (<link>)
- Simplification: how far from the subject; what is left
- Construction: grid, angles, curves, modules
- Silhouette: closed or open; how it reads at 16 px
- Figure and ground: is there a second shape in the white?
- Closure: what the viewer completes
- Terminals: cut ends, rounded ends, pixel steps
- Small size: what survives, what does not
- Metaphor: how meaning is carried (likeness, cause, convention)
- Can take: <principles, each one line>
- Must not copy: <the shapes and signatures that belong to this work>
```

Write claims about the work as sourced (the page you read) or as your inference.

## Similarity check

Before G6, search again with subject x same operation ("cat face negative space terminal"), and look at the results next to the chosen mark. Report what you found. The conclusion is always worded: "I found no similar mark with the tools I can use. This is not a legal trademark search." When the mark will be registered, the TM gate follows (interview.md, "Formal use and the TM gate").
