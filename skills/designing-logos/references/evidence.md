# Evidence: what research supports, and what it does not

Read this when a claim about how people perceive a mark goes into a rationale or an answer to the user. Most findings are lab or survey results whose effect depends on the brand and context. Use them to set up choices, never to overrule the user's pick.

Status: abstract read means the publisher's abstract was opened; secondary means only an encyclopedia, report or summary was seen; to verify means the claim must be checked against the source before it is quoted.

## Behind the round-1 pairs

| Source | Finding | Status | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jiang, Gorn, Galli and Chattopadhyay 2016, Journal of Consumer Research 42(5) | Circular logos evoke softness, angular ones hardness, and shift judgments of the product | Abstract read | The round or angular pair |
| Bar and Neta 2006, Psychological Science 17(8) | People generally prefer curved objects to sharp-angled ones | Abstract read | The round or angular pair |
| Cian, Krishna and Elder 2014, Journal of Marketing Research 51 | Static logos that imply motion feel more dynamic and engaging; the fit with the brand matters | Abstract read | The static or dynamic pair |
| Luffarelli, Stamatogiannakis and Yang 2019, Journal of Marketing Research 56(1) | Asymmetric logos help only brands with an exciting personality | Secondary | The static or dynamic pair |
| Luffarelli, Mudra and Mahmood 2019, Journal of Marketing Research 56(5) | Logos that show what the brand does rate higher; small effect for familiar brands | Abstract read | The figurative or letter pair |
| Henderson and Cote 1998, Journal of Marketing 62(2) | 195 logos, 13 characteristics: marks meant to be recognized do best when natural, harmonious and moderately elaborate | Abstract read | Abstraction stops before the subject is lost |

No study shows that these three pairs span all perceived differences between logos; the eight corners are a morphological box (Zwicky) used as a default, to be checked against what users pick.

## Claims not used

- Famous marks "built on the golden ratio": the circles are usually drawn on afterwards.
- "The simpler the better": moderate elaboration did best (Henderson and Cote). The checkable version is "readable at 16 px".
- "Round is friendly, angular is aggressive" as a law: the effect depends on what the brand wants.
- "Symmetric is always better" or "asymmetric is more modern": true only for certain personalities.
- Color psychology lists ("red means passion"): weak evidence, strong context effects.
- "Everyone sees the FedEx arrow": many never do. A hidden meaning is a bonus, never the basis of recognition.
